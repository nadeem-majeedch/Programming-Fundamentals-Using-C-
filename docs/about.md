## Educational philosophy

The course follows six teaching principles:

1. **No assumed knowledge.** The course starts from "what is a program?" —
   students who have never written code are the primary audience.
2. **Plain language first, terminology second.** Every concept is explained
   in everyday words before its formal name is introduced — and every formal
   term is defined again in the [Glossary](glossary.md).
3. **Learn by doing.** Reading is never enough: every concept comes with
   examples to run, exercises to solve, a lab to build, and bugs to hunt.
4. **Self-study by design.** Worked solutions, full answer keys, and hint
   ladders mean a student can verify their own progress without waiting for
   an instructor.
5. **Fundamentals before features.** The course teaches portable, modern C++
   only as fast as the underlying ideas require — no advanced feature before
   its foundation exists.
6. **Honest assessment.** Quizzes and rubrics tell you what you actually
   know, not what you hope you know — that honesty is what makes self-study
   work.

## The course

- **Title:** Programming Fundamentals Using C++
- **Scope:** 16 units · 32 sessions · examples, exercises, labs, quizzes,
  debug activities, challenges, revision sheets, and three projects
- **Audience:** absolute beginners through early-intermediate programmers
- **Licence:** [MIT](https://github.com/nadeem-majeedch/Programming-Fundamentals-Using-C-/blob/main/LICENSE) — free to study, adapt, and share with
  attribution
