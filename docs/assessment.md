# Assessment

> How you measure your progress · [Course home](index.md) · [Grading rubrics](grading.md)

This is a self-study course: **nothing here is graded by a server or an
instructor**. "Assessment" means a system that tells *you* — honestly —
whether you are ready to move on. Instructors who adopt the course can use
the same components and [rubrics](grading.md) directly.

---

## The assessment components

| Component | Count | Purpose | Self-check mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Exercises** | 8–12 per unit | daily practice | predict-the-output answers included; coding tasks verifiable by compiling |
| **Labs** | 1 per unit (01–15) | hands-on synthesis | published solution + [lab rubric](grading.md#lab-rubric-all-labs) |
| **Quizzes** | 10 questions × 15 units | concept verification | full answer key, explanations included |
| **Self-assessment system** | 34 instruments × ~11 questions | scheduled testing: weekly, topic, cumulative, final | hidden answer keys, explanations, scoring bands — [Self-Assessment Hub](self-assessment/index.md) |
| **Projects** | 3 (Weeks 5, 10, 16) | integration | rubric per project in [Grading](grading.md#project-rubrics) |
| **Practice final** | 20 questions (Unit 16) + 2 comprehensive practice finals ([A](self-assessment/final-exam-1.md) and [B](self-assessment/final-exam-2.md), 25 questions each) | course-wide review | full answer key |

## How each component works

### Exercises (every session)

Three kinds, always in this order of difficulty:

1. **Concept checks** — vocabulary and "explain in your own words" questions.
2. **Predict the output** — write your answer *before* running; correct
   answers for every item are collected at the bottom of the sheet.
3. **Short coding tasks** — small programs from a spec; the compiler is your
   first checker.

### Labs (every unit)

1. Read the lab brief and requirements.
2. Build your solution from the starter (or from scratch — both count).
3. Compare your output with the expected output in the brief.
4. Open the published solution **only after your attempt** — then diff
   against it. Different-but-correct is fine; the rubric decides.
5. Score yourself with the [lab rubric](grading.md#lab-rubric-all-labs).

Each lab also contains a **Debug It** file: a program with seeded bugs to
find and fix before looking at the fix list.

### Quizzes (every unit)

- 10 questions, four types: multiple choice, true/false, predict-the-output,
  find-the-bug.
- **Attempt all 10 before opening the answer key.** The key explains *why*,
  not just *what* — reading it is the lesson.
- Score with the bands below.

### Projects (Units 05, 10, 16)

Projects are bigger, multi-session pieces of work with a brief, starter
code, and a solution. Score them with the project rubrics in
[Grading](grading.md#project-rubrics). In self-study mode the rubric is a
mirror, not a judge: it tells you what to improve before you call the
project done.

## Self-scoring bands

| Score | Verdict | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **≥ 90%** | Ready | Continue to the next unit or component. |
| **70–89%** | Almost | Re-read the sections your wrong answers point to; retake after a day. |
| **< 70%** | Not yet | Redo the unit's exercises + revision sheet, re-run the examples, retake. |

**Progress rule:** a unit is complete when its lab works (rubric-checked)
and its quiz is ≥ 90%. The course's recommended sequence depends on it —
later units assume earlier ones.

## Recommended record-keeping

Keep a single progress file (paper or digital). Suggested columns:

```text
Unit | Quiz score | Lab rubric score | Weak spots | Date
 01  |    9/10    |      92/100      | endl vs \n | 2026-10-01
```

Weak spots feed your revision before the next unit — and the practice final
in Unit 16 reviews them all.

---

## Honesty policy (the honour system)

The solutions are published on purpose. The system only works if you:
attempt before checking; build before diffing; score honestly. "Finishing"
pages is easy; the quiz bands and rubric lines are what actually protect
your future self — in Unit 16 and in every program you write after the
course.
